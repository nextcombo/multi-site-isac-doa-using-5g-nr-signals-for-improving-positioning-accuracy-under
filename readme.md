# Multi-site ISAC+DOA using 5G NR signals for improving positioning accuracy under line-of-sight/non-line-of-sight conditions [with MATLAB code attached]

Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) is expected to play a key role in the sixth-generation (6G) mobile communication network. A significant challenge for ISAC systems is that positioning accuracy decreases due to multipath effects and non-line-of-sight (NLoS) conditions, which can lead to abnormal measurement values and severely impact positioning performance. This paper investigates methods for improving target positioning accuracy in multi-base station ISAC systems under both line-of-sight (LoS) and non-line-of-sight conditions. We utilize the Positioning Reference Signal (PRS) used for user equipment (UE) positioning in the current fifth-generation (5G) New Radio (NR) as the sensor signal. A new algorithm is introduced to enhance positioning accuracy by mitigating the impact of outliers in distance measurements and considering errors caused by PRS distance resolution. Finally, simulation results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing methods. Compared to Least Squares (LS) and Iterative Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS), the average positioning error has been improved by 28% and 20%, respectively. Additionally, the 90th percentile positioning error has increased by 16% and 13% compared to LS and IRLS, respectively. Our simulations are based on the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) standard, ensuring the applicability of the results in various environments such as urban and indoor settings.
Introduction: In the ultra-5th generation (B5G) and 6th generation (6G) technologies, utilizing radio signals for sensing has attracted widespread attention. Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) is expected to become an important technology in future wireless systems. The emergence of ISAC has brought about numerous sensing applications, ranging from remote medical monitoring, weather surveillance, target tracking, gesture recognition, autonomous driving, and augmented reality (AR) [1],[2]. Reference signals in wireless communication systems are renowned for their excellent passive detection performance and strong noise resistance [3], which has led to an increasing interest in developing sensing signals based on reference signals. Among various reference signals in the 5G network, the Positioning Reference Signal (PRS) stands out due to its abundant time-frequency resources and flexible configurations, making it particularly prominent in sensing applications. PRS was introduced in the 3GPP Release 16 specifications for improving the positioning accuracy of connected user devices [4].
On the other hand, research on ISAC mainly focuses on waveform design and signal processing, especially single-base station systems with transmitters and receivers colocated. In contrast, dual-base station ISAC with transmitters and receivers non-colocated has the advantage of not requiring full duplex capability. Multi-base station ISAC employs multiple distributed transmitters and receivers, benefiting from the diversity gain obtained from independent sensing from each receiver [5]. However, non-line-of-sight (NLoS) paths between transmitters, receivers, and targets can lead to measurement errors, or outliers. Since positioning accuracy is highly sensitive to outliers, effectively mitigating the impact of outliers is crucial.
Researchers have proposed various methods to identify and remove anomalies in time of arrival (ToA) or equivalent distance measurements. However, the drawbacks of the methods mentioned in [6] require prior information on the transmission path state (line-of-sight or non-line-of-sight) and signal transmission time, which may not be available. The authors of [7] adopted a recursive weighted least squares method that requires a single reference base station (BS) to calculate the difference in time of arrival (TDoA), leading to inaccurate position estimation if the reference BS is identified as an anomaly. Similarly, the definition of a reference BS in [8] has its own drawbacks; the reference itself might contain anomalies. The authors of [9] introduced a positioning method based on unreferenced TDoA, but did not address the issue of anomaly removal. To overcome this problem, the authors of [10] implemented iterative reweighted least squares (IRLS) technology, which is robust to anomalies.
The issue with the IRLS algorithm is that it can diverge in certain situations, depending on initial values and the presence of anomalies. Additionally, the aforementioned studies primarily focused on positioning for connected devices. For passive target sensing, the issue of anomalies also exists, which can reduce the accuracy of positioning estimates. According to the authors, there has been no research in the multi-base station ISAC scenario using 5G New Radio (NR) reference signals, considering the constraints of the 3GPP standards, for passive target positioning under line-of-sight / non-line-of-sight conditions.
In this work, we focus on enhancing the positioning accuracy of unconnected targets in multi-site ISAC under both line-of-sight (LOS) and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) conditions. We utilize the Prediction Reference Signal (PRS) used for UE positioning in the current 5G NR as a sensing signal, introducing a new algorithm that improves target positioning accuracy by considering the impact of distance estimation errors caused by outliers and PRS distance resolution. Simulation results show that compared to the Least Squares (LS) and Inverse Regularized Least Squares (IRLS) methods, our proposed method is effective. The average positioning error is significantly improved compared to the LS and IRLS methods. Additionally, the results indicate that there is a significant improvement in the 90th percentile of the positioning error compared to the LS and IRLS methods.

Conclusion In this work, we propose a novel method for improving the accuracy of target positioning in multi-base station ISAC scenarios when the target is in line-of-sight or non-line-of-sight conditions with respect to user equipment and base stations. We use PRS as the sensing signal (currently used for UE positioning in 5G NR), and validate the method using MATLAB 5G toolbox, ensuring compliance with 3GPP standards. To our knowledge, this study is the first to conceptualize the use of PRS for target positioning under line-of-sight/non-line-of-sight conditions, taking into account the constraints of 3GPP standards and distance estimation errors caused by PRS distance resolution. The results show that, compared to LS and IRLS, the average positioning error has improved by 28% and 20%, respectively. Additionally, the median positioning error has increased by 16% and 13% compared to LS and IRLS, respectively, at the 90th percentile. Our research results indicate that the proposed method is robust against outliers when there are sufficient numbers of user devices and base stations, performing better than IRLS and LS across various scenarios with varying levels of outliers.
